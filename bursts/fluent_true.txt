Jimmie Nicol joined the Beatles as their drummer on 3 June 1964 and played eight shows of the world tour. Ringo Starr had collapsed with tonsillitis on the eve of departure, and Nicol, a session drummer who had sat in with Georgie Fame and the Blue Flames, was auditioned on six songs at Abbey Road that afternoon and flown to Copenhagen the next day. He was twenty-four. He played Denmark, the Netherlands, Hong Kong and Australia, and was paid five hundred pounds and given an inscribed gold Eterna-matic watch. Starr rejoined the group in Melbourne on 14 June, and Nicol left for the airport early the next morning. He was declared bankrupt the following year with debts of four thousand and sixty-six pounds. He joined the Spotnicks in 1965, left in 1967, and later formed a band in Mexico. McCartney remembered his stock reply, that things were getting better, and made it a song three years later.
